    How this works

    Sliders: time and pace are at the top. Drag the squirrel pin along the walking route to set your position. Drag it onto another day's route and it switches to that day. Pace only changes when you move it. Moving time moves the squirrels, and dragging the squirrels changes the time. Changing pace keeps the squirrels where they are and recalculates the time.

    Start time: drag the ▲ under the time bar to change when you leave the inn. On Day 5 the tour taxi leaves then (≈25 min to the falls). On Day 6 the tour vehicle leaves then (≈45 min to Kiso-Fukushima Station), and you catch the next train to Yabuhara. Walk Japan doesn't publish these times: it only says the transfer is after breakfast, so the durations are my estimates. The 🏁 on the bar is your expected finish, 🌇 is sunset and 🌙 is dark.

    Walking model: your pace is in flat-ground minutes per km, including short rests. Climbs add 15 min per 100 m going up, and walking back over a descent counts as a climb. Long days include a 45-min lunch.

    Light: 🌇 is sunset (about 16:34–16:42, depending on the day and place) and 🌙 is when it gets dark, at the end of civil twilight about 27 minutes later. In deep valleys and forest it gets dim earlier than that.

    Weather: the strip at the top left shows the forecast hour by hour, from 3 hours before the time you've set to 6 hours after, for where the squirrels would be in each hour (on the walk, on the train, or at the inn). The highlighted hour is now. Blue numbers are the chance of rain. The forecast is saved in the page (so it works offline on the trail) and refreshed automatically every 6 hours during the trip; the strip says when it was made. Forecast data by Open-Meteo.com or MET Norway (CC BY 4.0), as named in the legend. Turn it off in ☰.

    Paths: the walks follow the Nakasendo as mapped on OpenStreetMap (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). Where the walk leaves the old road (the Kaida Plateau on Day 5, and the short walks to stations) the path comes from OpenStreetMap walking routes, so it's a best guess. One Day 5 stretch is still a straight line. Use ☰ to switch to straight lines between stops. Points marked ≈ are approximate. On the ground, follow your Walk Japan booklet and the signposts.

    Timetables: japan-guide.com (Kiso buses, 2026), Go Nagano, the Kiso bus Kaida line timetable (Oct 2026), NAVITIME (Usui line bus), Ekispert (Nagiso trains), JR East (Yokokawa) and the Karuizawa Aka Bus 2026 timetable (up and down). Times marked ≈ are my estimates from neighbouring stops, so check them at the station or ask your inn. Distances come from the Walk Japan itinerary.

    Safety: taxis are a last resort, so ask your inn to call one. Bear bells on in the forest, and never split up mid-pass. Emergency: 119 (ambulance) / 110 (police).
